Part II · The Plumbing

When the government borrows, spends or taxes, the dollars don't appear or vanish. They move between a handful of big accounts, and most of those accounts sit in one place: the Federal Reserve.

The Fed's balance sheet is the centre of the map. On the left are its assets, mostly Treasuries and mortgage bonds. On the right is what it owes: paper currency, banks' reserves, the Treasury's checking account and overnight deposits from money funds. The two columns are always the same height. That's not a policy, it's accounting.

The Treasury General Account is the government's checking account at the Fed. When taxes come in or new bonds are sold, it fills up. Because Fed assets don't change, something else on the right has to shrink, usually bank reserves. When the government spends, the money flows back.

The overnight reverse repo facility used to be a second reservoir: money funds parked up to there in late 2022. It is now essentially empty (). That's what "the RRP is drained" means: new drains now come straight out of bank reserves.

Below the Fed sits the repo market, a pawn shop for bonds. Money funds lend cash overnight against Treasuries. Dealers pass it on to hedge funds that hold Treasuries with borrowed money. If private cash runs short, the Fed's standing repo lends at the top of its rate corridor.

How to read the map. The two Fed columns are drawn to scale, from H.4.1 for Wednesday Oct 28, 2026. Each circle's area is proportional to one dollar figure, named in its panel: Treasury holdings for most holders, deposits for households, repo borrowing for hedge funds, token supply for stablecoins. Those dates differ (Z.1 is Q2 2026). Click any bucket, or tab to it and press Enter, for details. Then pick a scenario below and step through it. Cash flows are drawn as filled dots and securities as hollow dots. Buckets that grow turn blue, buckets that shrink turn rust.

Scenarios

Every step changes the buckets above, and the page checks after each step that Fed assets still equal Fed liabilities (and that bank reserves and deposits move together). Sizes are round, illustrative amounts; the starting point is today's actual balance sheet.

Codex

Every term in the text. fact = an accounting or legal definition. heuristic = market shorthand, useful but not an official measure.

Sources & methodology

Every figure on this page comes from one data object at the top of this file (a copy of data/data.json). Each figure has a value, a date, a source and a status. primary = read directly from the source. secondary = a site citing the source, not yet checked against it. derived = computed from other figures.
